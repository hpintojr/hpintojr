# XBeton: one company, three useful conversations

## Package story arc

A building begins as a vision, but the wall system shapes the decisions that follow. XBeton offers AAC blocks and reinforced panels, supported by the Bond & Shield product family and project-specific conversations. The package connects an attractive finished home to the material and assembly behind it, then gives each audience a concrete next step.

Homeowners need to understand comfort, design possibilities, resilience and the scope of a useful cost comparison. Builders need to understand what changes in product selection, handling, crew preparation and the installed quote. Specifiers need to connect design intent with current product information, applicable assembly evidence and responsible engineering review.

The proof changes with the question. Website architecture is inspiration, not proof of a completed XBeton project. Authentic block, panel and packaging images explain the product system. The FAQ supplies orientation and supplier-reported ranges, not final project design values. Quantified marketing claims carry their source and limitations. The owner-selected six-hour fire claim remains visibly marked [NEEDS VERIFY], because current marketing and the FAQ differ and the underlying report is not in the reviewed source set.

All three decks close on a project conversation. This is the common conversion path: make the audience's next question easier to ask, then collect enough project context to route it correctly. Company positioning appears in each deck without adding an investor or internal sales-platform deck.

## Audience narratives

| Audience | Problem and stakes | XBeton answer | Proof emphasis | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Homeowner | An appealing design still needs informed wall-system decisions | AAC explained through comfort, material choice and project planning | Labeled architecture, authentic products, complete cost scope | Discuss my project |
| Builder | An unfamiliar system introduces scope and coordination questions | A product-and-installation planning conversation | Product formats, crew/logistics checklist, supplier labor claim and proposed pilot measurements | Request a project review |
| Specifier | Generic benefit claims are insufficient for a specification | Product, assembly and detail review | Supplier ranges, evidence requests, document revision and applicability | Request a technical review |

## Cross-mix and reuse

- Product family content repeats at Homeowner 4, Builder 3 and Specifier 3. The same authentic block/panel/Bond & Shield images and terminology create continuity.
- Fire evidence repeats at Homeowner 6, Builder 8 and Specifier 5, with audience-appropriate explanations and identical visible verification status.
- Closing QR destinations repeat at slide 10 in all decks. The language changes to match the audience, while products, inspiration/project resources and contact remain one navigation system.
- Homeowner 9 brings the builder and designer into the decision. Builder 8 carries the homeowner's comfort and performance outcomes into execution. Specifier 2 and 7 connect requirements to the builder's coordination work.
- The common four content pillars are transformations/inspiration, AAC education, process and craft, and brand/lifestyle. The homepage video and inspiration images remain clearly labeled architectural visualization.
- Layout, logo placement, palette, typography, citations and warning treatment repeat. Costs, field coordination, and technical-document requests stay specific to the relevant audience.
- Reuse the system for another brand by changing the brand variables, evidence register, approved assets and CTA destinations before regenerating. Never carry XBeton claims or imagery into a new client automatically.
